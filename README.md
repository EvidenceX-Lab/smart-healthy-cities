# Making Cities Smarter and Healthier using Artificial Intelligence, Data Science and Digital Transformation

## Overview

This repository represents the overarching research programme:

**Making Cities Smarter and Healthier using Artificial Intelligence, Data Science and Digital Transformation**

The project investigates how **Artificial Intelligence (AI), Data Science, digital technologies, and responsible innovation** can contribute to smarter, healthier, safer, more equitable, and better-governed cities.

The programme brings together complementary research on:

- environmental justice and urban inequality;
- probabilistic artificial intelligence and urban risk;
- active travel and urban safety;
- responsible artificial intelligence;
- governance and institutional decision-making;
- data-driven urban analytics;
- digital transformation; and
- evidence-informed decision support.

The research is conducted at **Birmingham City University** under the direction of **Prof. Muhammad Afzal, Project Director**.

The project is supported through the **International Science Partnerships Fund (ISPF)** and delivered through the **British Council**.

## Research Vision

Cities are increasingly complex, data-rich environments in which environmental conditions, mobility, public safety, infrastructure, social inequalities, digital technologies, and governance interact.

Artificial Intelligence and Data Science provide significant opportunities to understand these interactions and support better urban decision-making. However, technological capability alone is insufficient.

Effective smart and healthy city systems must also consider:

- equity;
- environmental justice;
- uncertainty;
- safety;
- transparency;
- accountability;
- responsible use of AI;
- institutional governance;
- evidence quality; and
- appropriate human oversight.

This project therefore investigates not only **how AI and data can make cities smarter**, but also how these technologies can contribute to cities that are **healthier, safer, fairer, and more responsibly governed**.

## Research Programme

The project consists of three complementary research streams.

### 1. Urban Environmental Justice

**Researcher:** Yeunsoo Park

#### Current Research Focus

**Environmental Justice Index for Smarter and Healthier Cities**

This research develops data-driven approaches for understanding, measuring, and analysing environmental inequalities within urban environments.

The work investigates how environmental conditions, socioeconomic characteristics, population vulnerability, and spatial factors can be integrated to construct transparent and reproducible indicators of environmental justice.

**Key research areas include:**

- Environmental Justice Index development;
- environmental exposure;
- environmental amenities;
- social vulnerability;
- spatial inequality;
- geospatial data science;
- composite indicators;
- urban health and equity;
- spatial analysis; and
- evidence-informed urban planning.

**Research Repository:**  
`EvidenceX-Lab/environmental-justice-index`

---

### 2. Probabilistic AI for Urban Risk

**Researcher:** Chulwoong Park

#### Current Research Focus

**Active Travel and Crime Risk Estimation through Probabilistic Artificial Intelligence**

This research develops probabilistic AI methods for uncertainty-aware urban risk modelling and decision support.

The work investigates how probabilistic models can represent spatial and temporal variation, uncertainty, and complex urban risk patterns, with an initial focus on crime-related risk and active travel.

**Key research areas include:**

- probabilistic artificial intelligence;
- urban risk modelling;
- crime-risk estimation;
- active travel;
- spatial-temporal modelling;
- uncertainty quantification;
- probability calibration;
- urban mobility;
- safer cities; and
- responsible decision support.

The research explicitly considers uncertainty rather than treating AI-generated risk estimates as deterministic predictions.

**Research Repository:**  
`EvidenceX-Lab/probabilistic-ai-urban-risk`

---

### 3. Responsible AI and Governance

**Researcher:** Kyounghee Cho

#### Current Research Focus

**Operationalising Responsible Artificial Intelligence in Governance**

This research investigates how responsible AI principles can be translated into practical governance mechanisms, institutional processes, assessment frameworks, and decision-making practices.

The work considers how organisations and public institutions can move from high-level principles of responsible AI toward mechanisms that can be implemented, evaluated, and governed in practice.

**Key research areas include:**

- responsible artificial intelligence;
- AI governance;
- accountability;
- transparency;
- fairness;
- human oversight;
- institutional governance;
- AI assurance;
- risk management;
- policy and regulatory frameworks; and
- responsible digital transformation.

**Research Repository:**  
`EvidenceX-Lab/responsible-ai-governance`

## How the Research Streams Connect

The three research streams address distinct but interconnected dimensions of smarter and healthier cities:

| Research Stream | Primary Question | Key Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Urban Environmental Justice** | Are environmental benefits, burdens, and vulnerabilities distributed equitably? | Environmental justice evidence and spatial indicators |
| **Probabilistic AI for Urban Risk** | How can AI represent urban risk and uncertainty? | Probabilistic models and uncertainty-aware decision support |
| **Responsible AI & Governance** | How should AI-enabled urban systems be governed? | Responsible AI frameworks and governance mechanisms |

Together, the streams provide complementary perspectives on the use of AI, data, and digital technologies in urban environments.

The programme connects:

**Urban Data → Evidence → AI & Analytics → Uncertainty → Equity → Governance → Decision-Making**

with the broader objective of supporting:

**Smarter • Healthier • Safer • Fairer • Responsibly Governed Cities**

## Shared Research Themes

### Artificial Intelligence

Development, evaluation, and responsible application of AI methods to complex urban problems.

### Data Science

Integration and analysis of heterogeneous spatial, temporal, environmental, social, behavioural, and institutional data.

### Digital Transformation

Understanding how digital technologies transform urban systems, institutions, services, and decision-making processes.

### Smart and Healthy Cities

Using technological innovation to support quality of life, health, safety, sustainability, accessibility, and urban resilience.

### Environmental Justice and Equity

Understanding how environmental benefits, burdens, risks, and resources are distributed across communities and locations.

### Uncertainty

Recognising that data and AI outputs contain uncertainty and ensuring that uncertainty is measured, communicated, and considered in decision-making.

### Responsible AI

Ensuring that AI systems and their use consider fairness, accountability, transparency, privacy, safety, appropriate human oversight, and societal impact.

### Governance

Investigating how public institutions and other organisations can effectively govern AI-enabled decision systems.

### Evidence-Informed Decision-Making

Supporting urban decisions with transparent, traceable, reproducible, and appropriately interpreted evidence.

## Project Objectives

The overall project seeks to:

1. Investigate how AI and Data Science can contribute to smarter and healthier cities.
2. Develop data-driven approaches for understanding environmental justice and urban inequality.
3. Investigate probabilistic AI approaches for uncertainty-aware urban risk estimation.
4. Examine how urban safety and risk relate to active travel and mobility.
5. Develop practical approaches for operationalising responsible AI in governance.
6. Investigate interactions between technological innovation, equity, uncertainty, responsibility, and institutional decision-making.
7. Develop reproducible research methods, datasets, analytical approaches, models, and evidence resources.
8. Produce academic publications and other research outputs contributing to AI, urban analytics, responsible innovation, and smart-city research.

## Data Management

Each research stream is responsible for documenting the datasets used in its research.

Dataset documentation should include, where relevant:

- dataset name;
- provider;
- original source;
- licence;
- date accessed;
- geographic coverage;
- temporal coverage;
- spatial resolution;
- variables used;
- preprocessing;
- transformations;
- access restrictions;
- authoritative storage location; and
- provenance.

Large, restricted, sensitive, confidential, copyrighted, or non-redistributable datasets should **not** be uploaded directly to GitHub unless redistribution is explicitly permitted.

GitHub repositories should preferentially contain:

- dataset documentation;
- metadata;
- schemas;
- small redistributable samples;
- processing code;
- configuration files; and
- provenance information.

## Reproducibility

Reproducibility is a core principle of the project.

Where technically, legally, and ethically possible, important research outputs should be traceable through:

> **Publication / Result → Analysis or Experiment → Model / Method → Configuration → Code → Data → Original Source**

Researchers should document:

- software versions;
- datasets and versions;
- model versions;
- analytical parameters;
- experimental configurations;
- random seeds where relevant;
- processing steps;
- important assumptions; and
- known limitations.

Publication-specific repository releases should be created where appropriate so that published outputs remain linked to the exact research artefacts used to generate them.

## Responsible AI and Responsible Research

Responsible AI applies across the entire programme and is not limited to the Responsible AI and Governance research stream.

The programme considers:

- fairness;
- environmental and social justice;
- transparency;
- accountability;
- privacy;
- security;
- uncertainty;
- explainability;
- spatial bias;
- data quality;
- representativeness;
- human oversight;
- model limitations;
- appropriate use; and
- responsible communication of research findings.

AI outputs, indices, maps, risk estimates, and analytical models should be interpreted within their methodological and evidential limitations.

Research outputs should support informed decision-making rather than being treated as unquestionable or deterministic representations of individuals, communities, places, or future events.
